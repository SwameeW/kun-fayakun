# Kun Fayakun: screenplay

A documentary film for a planetarium dome, played in the browser. About 25 minutes: eleven chapters
(22 min 18 s), a four-second title card before each, and two minutes of end credits over the CMB sky.
The shot list in [shotlist.json](shotlist.json) is the timing source; the tables below are generated
from it. Every rule in docs/SPEC.md sections 1, 2, 5, 6 and 9 overrides anything here.

**Narration.** No voice (owner's decision, 2026-10-23). The narration script below is shown as DOM
captions in the lower third, in the three-layer order the panel uses: what the text says, how scholars
have understood it, what science measures. It never claims proof. Quran recitation, when it comes
(M4), is real recitation from the licensed source; during it, captions and effects pause and the
camera holds.

**Respect rules that shape the cut.** Verses fade in and out whole, on their own cards in a fixed
reading area, never on objects that move through, explode or turn to particles. No scene event fires
during a verse or in the two seconds before it, so nothing is timed to land on a word. Divine acts are
shown only through light, silence, calligraphy and the cosmos. No hands for 39:67. Scripture and hadith
come only from data/quran.json and data/hadith.json.

**Lenses.** Focal lengths are for a full-frame sensor (24 mm high). Chapters 0 to 6 were built for a
50-degree view, the 26 mm equivalent; Chapters 7 to 10 choose lenses per shot (22 mm for scale, 85 mm
for the Moon).

**Grades by act.** Act I white-hot to amber; Act II deep blue and violet; Act III warm earth and ocean;
Finale soft white.

## Cold open (Chapter 0, first 17.5 s)

Absolute black and silence for four seconds. Then the word kun (from 36:82, set in Amiri Quran from the
Tanzil text) forms in light over at least four seconds and breathes. Title card: "Kun Fayakun". The
calligraphy never breaks into particles.

## Act I. The Beginning (Chapters 0 to 2)

From before time to the first light. 2:117 and 36:82 are read over the still word, and it stays alight, breathing, for two
short hadith cards, each a sourced summary labelled as one (the wording awaits entry by a person),
with its references: Imran ibn Husain's hadith with Abdullah ibn Amr's, then the hadith of the Pen with
both of its gradings side by side; narrators, notes and links are in the notes. Only then does its light
fade, and in the darkness: "Whether anything came before the beginning of time lies beyond what physics
can test." A point of light grows softly;
space stretches, carrying the dust with it, white-hot cooling to amber; 21:30 is read over the
condensing plasma. One unbroken push carries the camera into the plasma of Chapter 2, where photons
wander until recombination clears the fog in one breath, and the real Planck sky appears around the
viewer before the camera draws back through the shell. 41:11 is read over the globe.

## Act II. The Building of the Heavens (Chapters 3 to 6)

The dark ages give way to the cosmic web, grown by the Zel'dovich approximation, and the first stars
swell softly in its knots. Among the galaxies, space stretches and their light reddens; 51:47 is read
in a quiet drift; both Hubble-constant values are shown and the tension is left unresolved. From an
artist's model of the Milky Way the view draws back through five powers of ten to real SDSS galaxies,
closes on the equatorial slice where the web is plainest, and ends inside the limit of the observable
universe; 67:3 and 65:12 are read over the web, which is never identified with the seven heavens. A
massive star, cut open to show its layers, collapses and is torn apart; its debris carries the elements
out, dimmed to a quiet glow under 57:25 so the verse never sits on the explosion; then a white dwarf and
its companion, and a Type Ia supernova whose light fades with the nickel-56 decay chain.

## Act III. A Home Is Prepared (Chapters 7 to 9)

### Chapter 7. The Sun and its course

A slow crane over a protoplanetary disk: dust lanes, and gaps cleared by growing planets. The Sun
begins to shine, eased, never a flash. The disk clears and the camera settles on the living Sun
(granulation, limb darkening, a corona carried by the bloom) for 36:38. The orrery opens: the planets on
their real orbits from NASA JPL's orbital elements, at their real positions for today's date, the
orbits drawn as fine engraved rings of warm light graduated like an astrolabe's limb; sizes and
distances are labelled not to scale. A long-lens hold beside the Earth watches the Moon pass through its
phases while 36:39 is read. Wide again for 36:40 and 21:33, each verse on its own card. Only after the
verses does the view pull back to the Sun's course around the Milky Way, with the caption that this is
science, not the meaning of the verse. The scholarly layer offers, quietly and never timed to a verse,
"Explore the letters of this phrase": the visitor-led letter analysis of kullun fi falakin.

Narration: "About 4.6 billion years ago, a cloud of gas and dust collapsed into a spinning disk around a
newborn star." "Within the disk, dust grew into ever larger bodies, and the growing planets swept lanes
clear around them." "At the centre the Sun began to shine, fusing hydrogen into helium." "The leftover
gas was blown away, leaving the planets on their orbits." "These are the planets’ real orbits and
positions, from NASA JPL data; sizes and distances are not to scale." "The Sun is moving too, carrying
the planets around the centre of the Milky Way about once every 230 million years." "This is the science
of the Sun’s motion. It is not presented as the meaning of the verse."

### Chapter 8. The earth spread out

The young Earth turns as a molten world: lava cracks, steam. A Mars-sized body strikes it in a glancing
blow (eased, exposure dipped); debris rings the Earth and gathers into the Moon. The crust cools and
darkens while 41:9 is read; rain falls and the first oceans spread while 41:10 is read. Plates move,
continents and mountains rise, clouds form under a blue limb. Only when the Earth is complete and simply
turning are 79:30, 79:31 and 79:32 read, one card each; nothing is timed to their words. The panel holds
all the verses on the six days and the measure of a Day (7:54, 32:4, 25:59, 50:38, 32:5, 70:4, 22:47),
the classical explanation that the four days include the first two, the commentators' reconciliation of
the order, and no "ostrich egg" reading of dahaha.

Narration: "About 4.54 billion years ago the young Earth was a world of molten rock." "Leftover bodies
from the disk still struck it as it grew." "A giant impact is thought to have thrown out the debris that
became the Moon, some 30 to 100 million years after the solar system began; the timing is debated." "The
debris gathered into the Moon." "As the surface cooled, water rained out of the steam; tiny zircon
crystals show there was liquid water by about 4.4 billion years ago." "Over billions of years, the
moving plates of the crust raised continents and mountain ranges." "The plates are still moving today."

### Chapter 9. Life from water

A macro view inside an early ocean: light filtering down through water, single cells drifting. 21:30 is
read in that calm. The camera rises through the surface to orbit; as oxygen rises the sky and the seas
shift colour, and later the land turns green. 24:45 is read over the living planet. The chapter ends
before the first humans.

Narration: "All known life depends on liquid water." "The earliest widely accepted traces of life are
about 3.5 billion years old. Older claims, up to about 4.1 billion years, are debated." "A 2024 genetic
study places the last common ancestor of all life today at about 4.2 billion years ago." "From about 2.4
billion years ago, oxygen made by microbes began to build up in the air. It rose for good about 2.2
billion years ago." "Much later, life spread from the water onto the land." "The story pauses here,
before the first humans."

## Finale. The Return (Chapter 10)

The cosmos as it is today. Three possible futures, each labelled a hypothesis and shown in its own
locked-off shot beside a figure of the scale factor over time: heat death, the Big Rip, the Big Crunch.
Calm galaxies while 81:1, 81:2, 82:1, 82:2, 39:67 and 14:48 are read, one card each. Then, labelled
"An artistic image of the heavens folding, not a depiction of the Last Day", the heavens gently roll
inward; everything resolves into calm light; the calligraphy of 21:104 appears in light, and the verse
is read on its card. Silence.

Narration: "Today the universe is still expanding, and the expansion is speeding up." "What comes next
depends on dark energy, which is not yet understood. Science offers possibilities, not certainties." "If
dark energy stays constant, galaxies drift ever farther apart and the stars burn out: a cold, dark
universe." "If it grows stronger, it could one day tear apart galaxies, stars and planets." "If it
weakens, as recent DESI results hint, the expansion could slow, and might even reverse into collapse."
"An artistic image of the heavens folding, not a depiction of the Last Day."

## End credits

Two minutes over the Planck CMB sky. It opens with the name Kun Fayakun, then every source,
dataset, licence and reviewer, from data/sources.json, data/hadith.json and the review record, and
the on-screen disclaimer.
